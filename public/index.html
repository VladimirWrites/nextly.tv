Two trackers have already taken your data with them.

Tracking services get acquired, pivot, or quietly switch off, and years of watch history goes with them. The problem isn't that they were badly run. It's that the history lived on their servers instead of yours.

nextly is built so that can't happen. The data is encrypted with a key that never leaves your device, so nobody — including whoever runs the server — can read it. How it works

You get a number, not an account

No email, no password, nothing to verify. nextly generates a 28-character account number that is both your login and your encryption key. Save it the way you'd save a password — it's the only way in, and nobody can reset it for you.

Track what you watch

Search a show, tap once per episode. nextly tells you what's next, what's waiting, and when the next episode airs. It counts rewatches separately, so a second time through doesn't overwrite the first.

It syncs, encrypted, to every device

Enter the same number on your phone and your library is there. Two devices editing at once merge per episode rather than overwriting each other. The server only ever sees ciphertext.

What makes it different

The server can't read your data

Your account number derives two separate values: a hash the server uses as a row key, and — from a different input — an AES-GCM key that stays in the browser. Whoever holds the database holds noise.

An export that outlives the app

One button produces plain JSON with every show name and episode spelled out, readable in any text editor. Each show carries its IMDb id, so the file can be re-imported anywhere — not just here.

Nothing is tracked

No analytics, no cookies, no third-party scripts, no ad network, no email list. There is no consent banner because there is nothing to consent to.

Open source, and self-hostable

The whole thing is a Cloudflare Worker, a small database, and plain JavaScript with no build step. Run your own copy and you don't have to trust anyone, including us.

It works on a train

Install it like an app. Your library and episode lists are stored on the device, so it opens and keeps working with no connection and syncs when you're back.

Rewatches actually count

Most trackers make you erase a show to watch it again. nextly records which time through you're on, and shows the previous run underneath the current one.

Your whole history, in one strip

Every show gets a barcode: one tick per episode, grouped by season. Filled means watched, one cyan tick marks what you'd play next, faint means it hasn't aired yet, and a half-height tick is an episode you saw on a previous time through.

It takes the space of a progress bar and tells you far more than a percentage can — which season you stalled in, the episode you skipped, how much is left.

Questions

What happens if I lose my account number?
Your data is gone, and nobody can recover it. That's the direct cost of the server being unable to read it — there's no password reset because there's nothing on our side to reset against. nextly makes you save the number before it lets you in, and the export button exists so you always have a second copy.
What if nextly shuts down?
Export your data — you get a plain JSON file with every show name and episode written out, plus IMDb ids so another service can identify them. The code is open source, so you can also run your own copy and keep using the same account number. This is the failure the whole app is designed around.
Where do the shows and episode lists come from?
TVmaze, called directly from your browser — so even the list of shows you look up never reaches our server. You can switch to TMDB with your own API key if you want better artwork.
Is it really free?
Yes, and there's no upsell. It stores a few kilobytes per person and runs no servers of its own beyond a Worker, so it costs almost nothing to operate. If that ever changes, the export button and the source code mean you're never stuck.
Can I use it on more than one device?
Yes. Enter the same account number anywhere. If two devices change things at once, nextly merges them episode by episode instead of letting one overwrite the other, so marking different episodes on your phone and laptop keeps both.
How do I know any of this is true?
You can read it. All of it is on GitHub, including the encryption and every request that leaves your browser. Worth knowing where that stops being enough: we also serve the JavaScript holding your key, so you're trusting that what's published is what's running. Hosting it yourself is the only way to close that gap, and it's a supported way to use nextly rather than a workaround.
Do you have my email address?
No. There's no field to type it into. We don't have your name, your email, or a password of yours — only a hash that identifies a row, and a blob we can't open.
