Privacy

Last updated 29 July 2026

The short version: nextly stores an encrypted blob it cannot read, and a hash that identifies which blob is yours. It has no idea who you are, and no way to find out.

What is stored on the server

  • An account hash. A SHA-256 hash derived from your account number. It's the row key. It cannot be reversed into your account number, and it cannot decrypt anything.
  • An encrypted blob. Your library — shows, watch marks, settings — encrypted in your browser with AES-GCM before it is sent. The key is derived from your account number and never leaves your device. Nobody operating this service can read the contents, and neither can anyone who obtains a copy of the database.
  • A timestamp of the last write, used to reconcile edits made on two devices.
  • Your IP address, briefly. When a new account is created, the IP address is written to a short-lived log used only to rate-limit account creation. Entries older than 24 hours are deleted automatically, and they are not linked to any account.

What is not stored

No email address, no name, no password, no phone number, no payment details. There is no field in nextly to type any of them into. There are no cookies, no analytics, no advertising or tracking scripts, no third-party embeds, and no profiling. That is why you were not asked to dismiss a consent banner.

What stays on your device

Your account number, a local copy of your library, and a cache of episode data are stored in your browser's own storage. Clearing your browser data removes them. They are never transmitted in readable form.

Show data comes from elsewhere

Episode lists, artwork and air dates come from TVmaze, or from TMDB if you supply your own API key. Your browser contacts them directly, so those requests never pass through nextly's server — which means nextly does not know which shows you look up. It also means those services see your IP address and the titles you request, under their own privacy policies.

Hosting

nextly runs on Cloudflare Workers with a Cloudflare D1 database. As the hosting provider, Cloudflare processes connection data such as IP addresses in order to serve requests and mitigate attacks, under its own terms. Cloudflare cannot read the blob either.

That connection data does not say what you were looking at. The pages that name something specific — a show, a person, a season, an episode — carry that name in the part of the address after the #, which browsers never send to a server. So a request for a show page arrives here as a request for /show, and there is no record anywhere, ours or Cloudflare's, connecting an address to a programme.

Legal basis and retention

nextly is operated from Berlin, Germany, so the GDPR applies. Storing your encrypted blob is necessary to provide the service you asked for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). The short-lived IP log exists for security and abuse prevention (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) and is deleted within 24 hours. Your blob is kept until you delete it.

Your rights, and their limits

Under the GDPR you have rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection. In practice:

  • Access and portability are built in — the export button in Settings produces your complete data as readable JSON, immediately, without asking anyone.
  • Erasure is built in — "Delete everything" in Settings removes the stored blob permanently.
  • We cannot help you with any of these on request, because we cannot identify which row is yours without your account number, and could not read it if we could. This is a deliberate limitation, not an evasion.

If you lose your account number

Your data becomes permanently unreadable, by you and by everyone else. There is no recovery process, because a recovery process would require us to be able to decrypt your data. Export a copy while you still can.

Changes
